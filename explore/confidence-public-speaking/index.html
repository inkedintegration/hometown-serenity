Confidence can mean different things in different moments. Speaking in a meeting, preparing a presentation, or expressing a preference are useful, specific places to start.

Hypnotherapy for confidence & public speaking

Instead of a broad goal such as “be more confident,” think of a situation you would like to approach differently. Perhaps you want to ask a question in a group, deliver a wedding toast, or explain an idea at work without rushing.

A clear example gives a practitioner something concrete to discuss with you. It also helps you distinguish preparation, skill building, and emotional support, which may each play a different part.

Questions to explore before a speaking event

  • Which part feels hardest: preparing, waiting to begin, being watched, or handling questions?
  • What do you notice in your thoughts and body at that moment?
  • What would a manageable first step look like?
  • What preparation or practice would still be useful alongside a session?

Where hypnotherapy fits in the conversation

Confidence, public speaking, and assertiveness appear among HMI's listed uses of hypnotherapy. That list is a guide to topics people explore, rather than proof that a particular outcome will follow.

Ask Ashley how she would approach your specific goal, what a session might involve, and how you could recognize useful progress. Hypnotherapy is not a substitute for learning your material or practicing the skill you want to use.

A somatic perspective

Ashley's broader approach pays attention to mind and body experience. In a conversation about public speaking, you might describe what happens as you anticipate taking the floor, rather than only naming the event you fear.

If fears are severe or are limiting daily life, ask a qualified mental health professional about appropriate assessment and care.

Common questions

Do I need an upcoming presentation?+

No specific event is needed to ask about confidence. An everyday example can be just as useful for explaining your goals.

Can one session guarantee confidence?+

No. Your goals, circumstances, and the approach all matter. Ask about expectations without assuming a fixed number of sessions or a guaranteed result.
