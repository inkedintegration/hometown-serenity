Sometimes the starting point is simply noticing how much you have been carrying. A conversation about stress can begin with the demands of an ordinary day.

Hypnotherapy for stress & relaxation

Stress may be the word you use for a crowded schedule, an upcoming change, or the feeling that you never quite switch off. It helps to describe a specific moment: the end of the workday, the start of a difficult conversation, or the transition into rest.

Consider what you would like more room for. That might be a pause before responding, a calmer evening routine, or a clearer understanding of what feels demanding.

How hypnosis is used in stress programs

Some clinical programs combine hypnosis with relaxation, guided imagery, and other coping approaches. Vanderbilt's Osher Center describes one such clinician-led stress-management program. Its format and clinical services are specific to that program and are not a description of Ashley's sessions.

Ask Ashley which methods she uses, how a session would relate to your goals, and whether any between-session practice would be appropriate for you.

Mind and body, in your own words

A somatic perspective includes attention to bodily experience. You do not need technical language or a self diagnosis. Describing what you notice, and when you notice it, can help you explain your experience.

Terms such as “nervous system regulation” should not be taken as a promise to reset your body or permanently remove stress. Ashley's Beyond the Static article offers her personal perspective on the topic.

Common questions

Is stress management the same as treating an anxiety disorder?+

No. Everyday stress goals and clinical treatment needs can overlap, but they are not interchangeable. Persistent anxiety, panic, or symptoms that affect daily functioning deserve assessment by a qualified healthcare professional.

Do I have to be good at relaxing?+

You can start by asking questions. Tell Ashley what feels comfortable or difficult and discuss the approach before deciding to book.
